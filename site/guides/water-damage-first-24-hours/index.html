Water Damage: What to Do in the First 24 Hours

A burst pipe or a leak can do a lot of damage quickly. What you do in the first day makes a real difference to the repair and to your insurance claim.

1. Make it safe

If water is near outlets, appliances or your electrical panel, turn off power to that area at the breaker, and only if you can reach the panel without standing in water. Stay out of rooms with sagging ceilings. If the water may be contaminated, such as a sewer backup, keep people and pets away from it.

2. Stop the water

For a plumbing leak, shut off the main water valve. Every adult in the house should know where it is. For a roof leak, catch the water in buckets and move belongings out of the way until the roof can be covered or repaired.

3. Document everything

Before you move or throw anything away, take photos and video of the damage: the source, the wet areas and any damaged belongings. Your insurance company will want to see what happened.

4. Call your insurance company

Report the damage and ask what your policy covers. In general, homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental damage such as a burst pipe, but often do not cover slow leaks that went on over time, and flooding from outside usually requires separate flood insurance. Policies differ, so your agent or adjuster is the one to confirm.

5. Start drying

Remove standing water if it is safe to do so, lift furniture off wet floors and get air moving with fans. Building materials that stay wet can start to grow mold within a day or two, which is why speed matters.

6. Call a restoration contractor

Water travels under flooring and inside walls where you cannot see it. A contractor can find how far it went, dry the structure properly, remove what cannot be saved and rebuild.

Reliant Consulting and Construction handles the whole job, from water removal to the finished repair, and works directly with your insurance company.

What not to do

  • Do not use a household vacuum to pick up water.
  • Do not leave wet rugs, books or boxes sitting on carpet or wood floors.
  • Do not wait several days to see if it dries on its own.
  • Do not throw away damaged items before they are photographed.
