How Long Does a Bathroom Remodel Take?

Many of the bathroom remodels we complete take about four weeks from demolition to final walkthrough. Here is where that time goes and what can stretch it.

The short answer

For a typical bathroom, plan on about four weeks of construction once work starts. A simple update with a new vanity, fixtures, flooring and paint is quicker. A large primary bathroom with a custom tile shower and plumbing moved to new locations takes longer.

The planning that happens before the first day of work matters just as much. Choosing tile, fixtures and a vanity ahead of time is the single best way to keep a remodel on schedule.

What happens in each phase

  • Planning and selections. We measure the room, agree on the layout and you choose materials. Anything with a long delivery time is ordered before work begins.
  • Demolition. Old fixtures, tile and damaged drywall come out. This is when we find out what is behind the walls.
  • Rough-in work. Framing changes, plumbing and electrical are done while the walls are open, followed by any required inspection.
  • Walls and waterproofing. Backer board and drywall go up and the shower area is waterproofed.
  • Tile. Floors, shower walls and niches are set and grouted. Tile needs time to cure before the next step, and it cannot be rushed.
  • Finish work. Vanity, countertop, toilet, fixtures, glass, trim and paint.
  • Final walkthrough. We go through the room with you and take care of anything that is not right.

What makes a bathroom remodel take longer

  • Moving plumbing. Relocating a toilet, shower or drain adds work under the floor.
  • Hidden water damage. Rot around a tub or toilet is common in older bathrooms. It has to be repaired before new finishes go in.
  • Custom tile work. Large showers, patterns and built-in niches take more time than a standard surround.
  • Special-order materials. Custom vanities, glass and some tile have long lead times.
  • Changes after work starts. Changing the plan mid-project usually means waiting on new materials.

How to keep your remodel on schedule

Make your selections early and stick with them. Ask your contractor for a written schedule before work begins, and ask which days the bathroom will be completely out of use so you can plan around it.
