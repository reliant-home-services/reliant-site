## Government and commercial contracting (researched Nov 9; sources: GSA, SAM.gov, Georgia DOAS, GT APEX Accelerator, bonding guides)
Realistic view: worthwhile as a second income stream, slow to start, paperwork-heavy. Start with local/state work and subcontracting, not big federal bids.
1. **Free help first:** GT APEX Accelerator (Georgia Tech's procurement centre, free one-on-one counselling and bid matching). Book an intro call.
2. **Registrations (free):** SAM.gov entity registration (get a UEI; takes weeks, needs legal name/address matching your records exactly),
   Georgia Procurement Registry + Team Georgia Marketplace (state), and each county/city/school-district vendor list
   (Pickens, Cherokee, Cobb, Fulton, Gilmer, Dawson). Local governments post many small repair/renovation bids on the Georgia Procurement Registry.
3. **Know the rules:** federal construction over $150,000 needs performance and payment bonds (Miller Act); $35k-$150k needs alternative protection.
   The SBA bond guarantee can help small contractors get bonded. Federal jobs usually bring prevailing-wage (Davis-Bacon) paperwork and insurance requirements.
4. **Best entry points for Reliant:** small repair/renovation contracts for cities, counties, schools, housing authorities; subcontracting to larger
   general contractors on government jobs; property management and insurance restoration (your restoration service); HUD/housing-authority rehab work.
5. **Set-asides:** small-business certification helps (and veteran/disadvantaged/woman-owned categories if they apply). Dylan/Mark: tell me if any apply.
6. **Automation:** I can set a daily/weekly search of SAM.gov, the Georgia Procurement Registry and local bid pages for construction in your area (NAICS 236118
   residential remodelers, 236220 commercial building, 238990 other specialty trades) and send you a short list. Free.
Needs from you: legal business name and address exactly as registered, EIN, NAICS preference, insurance/bonding capacity, whether any set-aside applies.
